Think back to the office pool or the March bracket taped to the break-room fridge. Five bucks in, a lot of trash talk, and a good story whether you won or not. That's the right spirit. The money was the price of admission, not a plan to get rich.

The math in our vig explainer says the same thing: over time, most bettors lose. Where betting is legal and you choose to do it, treat it like that office pool.

Five rules that keep it fun

  1. Set a monthly budget you could lose completely without it hurting. Rent, bills, savings and debt money never go in the pot.
  2. Keep each bet small. A lot of people stick to 1 or 2% of their budget so one bad Sunday can't sink the month.
  3. Don't chase. Doubling up to "get it back" is how a hobby turns into a problem.
  4. Write it down. Like keeping score in a scorebook, a record of every bet shows the real result, which is usually worse than memory says.
  5. Use the brakes. Licensed sportsbooks offer deposit limits, time limits, cool-off periods and self-exclusion. Set them before you need them.

When it stops being fun

  • Betting more, or more often, than you planned
  • Borrowing, or using bill money
  • Hiding it from family or friends
  • Feeling restless or short-tempered when you try to cut back
  • Thinking about it most of the day

If any of that sounds familiar, please talk to someone. Call or text 1-800-GAMBLER (1-800-426-2537). It's free, confidential and open 24/7. There's more on our responsible gambling page.
