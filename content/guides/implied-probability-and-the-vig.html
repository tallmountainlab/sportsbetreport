Remember doing batting averages by hand? Hits divided by at-bats, worked out on the back of a baseball card or a school notebook. If you could do that, you can do this. Every betting price is a percentage wearing a disguise.

The formula

For a minus moneyline (a favorite): the number, divided by the number plus 100. For -150: 150 ÷ 250 = 60%.

For a plus moneyline (an underdog): 100, divided by the number plus 100. For +130: 100 ÷ 230 = 43.5%.

Why it adds up to more than 100%

60% plus 43.5% is 103.5%. That extra 3.5% is the house's cut, the vig, and it's how the book gets paid no matter who wins. It's the same idea as the carnival game at the county fair: you might win the giant stuffed bear, but the game was priced so the booth comes out ahead. A standard -110 / -110 spread works out to 52.4% on each side, so you'd have to win about 52.4% of those bets just to break even.

Taking the vig out

Divide each side by the total. Here: 60 ÷ 103.5 = 58% and 43.5 ÷ 103.5 = 42%. That's the "Win %" column in our morning report: the market's honest read on each team's chances, with the house's cut set aside.

Why it's worth knowing

Because of that cut, the average bet loses over time. That's not a reason to worry; it's just the price of the ticket, like the quarters you fed the arcade machine at the bowling alley. Knowing the math keeps expectations honest. More on that in setting a budget and playing responsibly.
